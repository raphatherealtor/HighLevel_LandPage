---
title: "Abuse and cost controls"
description: "Rate limits, bot defenses, provider caching, budget caps, and graceful degradation"
---

The public address-lookup endpoint is both expensive and exposed. These controls are required before preview traffic reaches a paid provider.

## Default lookup limits

- Maximum **3 distinct uncached property lookups per anonymous session per 24 hours**.
- Maximum **10 uncached lookups per IP per hour** and **30 per IP per 24 hours**.
- Repeated requests for the same normalized address use the permitted cached result and do not consume another paid lookup.
- Limits are server-side configuration, not browser-only checks.
- Rapha may change limits after observing legitimate traffic, cost, shared-network false positives, and provider terms.

## Bot mitigation

- Use a hidden honeypot field on the address form.
- Require a server-verified bot challenge such as Turnstile before a paid provider call. Prefer a managed or invisible interaction when risk is low.
- Reject malformed, automated, or replayed requests before provider invocation.
- Apply request-body size limits, schema validation, and timeouts.
- Never reveal whether an address belongs to a CRM contact or private record.

## Cache-before-provider policy

1. Normalize the address server-side.
2. Check a provider-specific cache only when the data contract permits caching and the configured TTL has not expired.
3. Coalesce concurrent requests for the same normalized address so one provider call serves all waiting requests.
4. Call the provider only after limits, bot verification, and budget checks pass.
5. Store only the minimum provider response allowed by contract.

<Warning>
  Caching is conditional, not assumed. If the property-data agreement prohibits caching, disable it and tighten rate and budget limits. Never cache proprietary MLS data without explicit rights and retention rules.
</Warning>

## Spend containment

- Configure `DAILY_LOOKUP_BUDGET_USD` from the selected provider price and approved launch budget.
- Alert Rapha at 50% and 80% of the daily budget.
- Stop new paid lookups at 100% unless Rapha explicitly raises the cap.
- Track provider calls, cache outcomes, rejected abuse, estimated spend, and cap events without logging unnecessary personal data.
- Keep preview/staging on a separate low budget or provider sandbox.

The approved budget value is a **Gate 0 blocker**. The implementation must not invent it.

## Cap and limit experience

When the budget or abuse limit blocks a lookup:

- Do not show a generic system error.
- Explain that instant reports are temporarily limited because of high demand.
- Offer retry timing when known.
- Offer a manual-review request through a path that does not invoke the paid lookup again.
- Do not promise a result until the manual workflow is verified.
